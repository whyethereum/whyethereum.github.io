Ethereum upgrade tracker

Where every network upgrade stands, in plain English. Status, features and dates are checked against developer call notes and official Ethereum Foundation posts.

Last updated November 19, 2026

Glamsterdam

Paused before final testnet

The next upgrade. It changes how blocks are built and processed so Ethereum can safely handle far more activity, with a planned gas limit of 200 million after launch.

Mainnet dateNot set yet
ExpectedEarly 2027
Test networksSepolia live · Hoodi on hold
Main blockerGas repricing review

What's in it

ProposalWhat it does
EIP-7732Enshrined proposer-builder separation (ePBS). Splits block building from block proposing inside the protocol, giving validators more time to check blocks.
EIP-7928Block-level access lists. Each block lists the data it touches, so computers can process transactions in parallel.
EIP-8037Reprices gas for creating new state, such as new storage slots and accounts, to keep the database from growing too fast.
EIP-8038Reprices gas for reading and writing existing state.
EIP-8159Networking update (eth/71) for how nodes share data.

Timeline

  • Mar–Sep 2026Developer test networks (devnets 0–11) run, ending with an 84,000-validator devnet in September.
  • Aug 17, 2026Platåberget public testnet launches. Mainnet target moves from the first half of 2026 to Q4 2026.
  • Oct 6, 2026Glamsterdam goes live on the Sepolia test network.
  • Oct 22, 2026Developers call off the Hoodi testnet fork planned for Oct 26 after auditors show the gas changes can alter how some contracts behave.
  • Nov 6, 2026The Ethereum Foundation opens a public review of eight contract assumptions the new gas model breaks. Hoodi waits until these risks are resolved.
  • NextDecide how to fix or adjust EIP-8037 and EIP-8038, then set new Hoodi and mainnet dates.

Sources: ethereum.org, Cointelegraph, Bankless, ACDE #248 notes, EF blog

Hegotá

Planning

The upgrade after Glamsterdam, named after Bogotá (execution layer) and Heze (consensus layer). It focuses on censorship resistance and better wallets.

Mainnet dateNot set yet
Target2027
Locked-in features2
StatusChoosing final scope

Locked in

ProposalWhat it does
EIP-7805FOCIL (fork-choice enforced inclusion lists). Lets many validators force transactions into blocks, so no single block builder can censor them.
EIP-8141Frame transactions. Native smart-wallet features such as paying fees in other tokens, plus an easier path to quantum-safe signatures.

Developers have called both features must-ship: they'd rather delay Hegotá than launch without them. More than a dozen other proposals are still being considered, including ideas for higher gas limits and more blob space.

Sources: ethereum.org, ForkLog, EF blog

Lean Ethereum (long-term roadmap)

Research & building

A multi-year rebuild of Ethereum's core, planned across about seven upgrades through 2029.

Quantum-safe targetDecember 2029
Next progress reviewJanuary 2027
Finality goalAbout 8 seconds
Throughput goalAbout 10,000 TPS on L1

The plan includes shorter block times, much faster finality, real-time zero-knowledge proofs of every block, and replacing today's signatures with quantum-resistant ones. Dates for most steps aren't set yet.

Sources: EF post-quantum roadmap, ForkLog, The Block

Fusaka

Live on mainnet

Shipped December 3, 2025. Its headline feature, PeerDAS, lets nodes check data by sampling small pieces, which made room for many more blobs (the cheap data space layer 2 networks use).

  • Dec 3, 2025Fusaka activates on mainnet.
  • Dec 9, 2025First blob increase: target 6 → 10 blobs per block, maximum 9 → 15.
  • Jan 7, 2026Second blob increase: target 14, maximum 21. No further increases are scheduled.

Sources: The Block, BlockEden
