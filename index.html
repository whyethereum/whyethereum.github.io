The case for ETH

Why people own ETH

ETH isn't just a coin. It's the fuel, the security deposit and the money of a network used by millions every day.

It fuels a busy network

Every transaction, trade and app on Ethereum pays its fee in ETH. Stablecoins, lending markets and tokenized funds from major financial firms settle on it, so demand for ETH grows with use.

It can earn a yield

Holders can stake ETH to help secure the network and earn new ETH in return, much like interest. You can stake through an exchange or run your own validator.

Part of every fee is burned

Since 2021, a portion of each transaction fee is destroyed forever. When the network is busy, more ETH is burned, which offsets the new ETH paid to stakers.

It's easy to get

Buy ETH on major exchanges and brokerage apps in most countries. In the US, you can also hold it through spot ETH exchange-traded funds in an ordinary brokerage account.

It keeps getting better

Hundreds of developers ship regular upgrades that make Ethereum faster, cheaper and more secure, with a public roadmap through 2029 that includes protection against quantum computers.

No one can switch it off

Ethereum is secured by validators spread across the globe. No single company or government controls it, which is the heart of its appeal.

What it is

One shared computer, thousands of apps

Think of Ethereum as an app store that no company owns. Developers build on it once, and anyone in the world can use what they build.

  • Send money worldwide in minutes, using ETH or dollar-pegged stablecoins.
  • Lend, borrow and earn through open financial apps with no bank in the middle.
  • Own digital items like art, tickets and game assets that you truly control.
  • Use faster, cheaper add-on networks (called layer 2s) that settle back to Ethereum for security.

Know the risks first

ETH can rise a lot, and it can fall a lot. Smart buyers go in with their eyes open.

  • The price is volatile. ETH has dropped more than 70% in past downturns.
  • Scams are common. No real project or company will ever ask for your recovery phrase.
  • Lost keys mean lost funds. There's no bank to call.
  • Rules vary by country, and taxes usually apply when you sell.
  • Only invest what you can afford to lose.
Questions

Common questions

What's the difference between Ethereum and ETH?

Ethereum is the network. ETH is the currency used on it to pay fees and secure the system.

How is Ethereum different from Bitcoin?

Bitcoin is built mainly as digital money. Ethereum is programmable, so developers can build apps, stablecoins, marketplaces and financial tools on top of it.

Do I need to buy a whole ETH?

No. You can buy a small fraction of one ETH, often for just a few dollars.

What is staking?

Staking means locking up ETH to help validate transactions. In return, you earn more ETH. You can stake through an exchange, a staking service, or your own validator with 32 ETH.

What is Glamsterdam?

It's Ethereum's next major upgrade. It changes how blocks are built and processed so the network can handle much more activity. It's being tested now.
